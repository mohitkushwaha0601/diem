Why it matters
Trade deals test whether India can widen export markets while protecting farmers, dairy producers and small manufacturers. The agreement also tests how far trade diplomacy with developed economies can deliver new market access.
India's stance
Official: the reports reviewed do not include a separate government statement beyond the entry into force. Inference: the government's case is likely to rest on market access, but the reports do not confirm this.
Competing viewpoints

07 / International · Europe & Fiscal Politics

France's riots prompt curfews and the interior minister stays, and the budget vote is postponed

In ~50 words: Weekend protests over living costs and education turned into riots, looting and arson across French cities. On 19 October, President Emmanuel Macron refused Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez's resignation, and Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu announced localised curfews. Reuters reports that the vote on the budget's revenue side was postponed from 20 October.

What happened?
Nuñez offered his resignation on Sunday and acknowledged that police "lost control" during the protests. France deployed 60,000 police and security forces on Saturday, which Nuñez said was not enough to control violent crowds in some cities. Paris police imposed an overnight curfew in two areas on Monday. Reports differ on the new date for the budget vote.
Why it matters
Riots that spread across cities test public order and state capacity. A budget vote delayed during unrest raises the question of whether fiscal measures can pass without public consent.
India's stance
Official: the reports reviewed contain no Indian government statement. Inference: India's interest is indirect, through European trade and investment and the health of a major partner's economy.
Competing viewpoints
The government says the curfews protect businesses from looting and destruction. Sharp criticism has focused on the police's handling of protests, and Macron has pointed to security failings. The reports reviewed do not show a completed inquiry.

SSB cue: "Reform without consent breeds unrest; say that governments must explain trade-offs, protect vulnerable groups and separate lawful protest from violence."

Why it matters

India's stance
Official: the reports reviewed contain no Indian government statement. Inference: India's role is mainly through surveillance, traveller advice and health diplomacy.
Competing viewpoints

SSB cue: "Outbreaks cross borders faster than rumours; say that India needs strong surveillance, quick reporting and funded regional cooperation, not panic."

01 / Geopolitics · West Asia & Energy

Trump says "no strike today" and weighs "a deal or finishing them off" as Qatari mediators reach Tehran

In ~50 words: On 25 October President Trump ruled out a US strike that day but told Fox News the question was whether to "make a deal with them or finish them off". Qatari mediators were in Tehran in an effort coordinated with Washington. Iran says messages continue through intermediaries; the US blockade and Hormuz sequencing remain the main obstacles.

What happened?
Since the 24 Oct brief, which led with US envoy Tom Barrack's remark that a weekend strike was "absolutely" possible, Trump said "there will not be a strike today" but did not rule out later action. Reuters, via Iran International, reports Qatari officials in Tehran as part of a US-coordinated push; Iran's foreign ministry spokesman said the diplomatic window is not closed while officials accuse Washington of demanding "complete surrender". Iran's ambassador to Pakistan said Tehran offered, via Islamabad, to reopen Hormuz if the US lifts its naval blockade, but the IRGC-linked Tasnim denied any new proposal. The Hill, citing sources, reports options for renewed strikes after the 3 November US midterms; this is a report of planning, not a decision.
Why it matters
The conflict, now in its seventh month by Reuters' count, has disrupted energy markets, and seven tankers were reported attacked or damaged around Hormuz on Friday. An IRGC brigadier general, Yadollah Javani, was also quoted by Bernama warning that cables under Hormuz would be destroyed if Iran's infrastructure were attacked, extending the cable-threat story from 24 Oct.
India's stance
Official: no Indian statement on the Trump-Iran exchanges was found; Indian officials have publicly urged dialogue and diplomacy on West Asia (see the UN Day item). Inference: India has high exposure through crude, shipping and the Gulf diaspora, so a deal that reopens Hormuz would serve its interest more than renewed strikes.
Competing viewpoints
Washington says it wants a permanent bar on Iranian nuclear weapons capability and keeps force as an option. Tehran argues the blockade and strikes are the cause of insecurity and rejects trading Hormuz for the blockade, according to Tasnim. Both claims are positions, not verified outcomes.

SSB cue: "In a coercive negotiation, deadlines and elections shape behaviour; say that India should keep backing mediation, protect energy supply lines and avoid taking sides on either side's maximal demands."

02 / International · West Asia & Indian Diaspora

Houthi missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabia continue; India again tells its nationals to follow local rules and not film interceptions

In ~50 words: On 25 October India's embassy advised nationals in Saudi Arabia to follow local rules and refrain from filming or publishing information about missile and drone interceptions. News18 reports Saudi Arabia intercepted 11 ballistic missiles and two drones within 12 hours. Riyadh has accused Iran of encouraging the Iran-backed Houthis; the claim is Saudi Arabia's, not an independent finding.

What happened?
The 24 Oct brief only noted reports that the government was assisting Indians stranded in Saudi Arabia; since then India has issued an explicit conduct advisory (The Hindu, 25 Oct). The context is a 10 October strike on King Khalid International Airport, Riyadh, which Saudi authorities said killed at least 13 and injured over 300 (CBS News). The Indian embassy reported on 15 Oct that one Indian was killed and 11 injured, and Indian carriers had suspended Saudi flights until 17 Oct (Firstpost). Saudi Arabia has accused Iran of encouraging the Houthis; Iran's role is not independently established in the sources reviewed.
Why it matters
The Houthi campaign shows how cheap drones and missiles can strain layered air defences and interceptor stocks; Secretary of State Rubio said in India that every country wants interceptors. It also threatens Red Sea shipping, Saudi energy sites and civilian aviation.
India's stance
Official: the embassy advises vigilance, following local authorities, and not sharing footage. Inference: with a very large Indian workforce in the kingdom and energy imports from the Gulf, India has strong reasons to keep neutral and prioritise evacuation readiness.
Competing viewpoints
Riyadh and Western governments describe the strikes as attacks on civilians and critical infrastructure, which the EU and Jordan condemned (ET Now report). The Houthis frame their attacks as part of a wider regional conflict; the sources reviewed do not set out the group's own account in detail.

SSB cue: "Protecting citizens abroad is a state duty with a citizen duty attached; say that India should keep advisories clear and consular capacity ready, and that individuals should follow local law and avoid sharing sensitive footage."

03 / India · Defence

On its 94th anniversary the IAF stages 'Wings Over Ganges' in Varanasi; its chief says theaterisation must not permanently split fighter assets

In ~50 words: PM Modi and Defence Minister Rajnath Singh watched the final day of the IAF's three-day 'Wings Over Ganges' show at Namo Ghat, Varanasi, on 25 October, with Tejas, Rafale, Su-30MKI, Prachand helicopters and Navy and Army aircraft. Separately, Air Chief Marshal A. P. Singh told PTI the IAF does not view theaterisation as permanently dividing fighter assets geographically.

What happened?
The display included the Surya Kiran aerobatic team and the Sarang helicopter team, and the participation of Navy and Army aircraft was presented as a show of jointness (ETV Bharat, Indian Express). In the PTI interview, the IAF chief called it his first comprehensive articulation on theaterisation and said air power's strengths are speed, reach and flexibility to concentrate combat power. Telegraph India adds that progress on the plan has stalled over differing service views on command structures; the sources reviewed do not report any decision on the proposal.
Why it matters
Theatre commands, announced in 2019 as the biggest military reform since Independence, are still unsettled. How fighter squadrons are assigned decides how quickly India can concentrate air power across the western and northern fronts.
India's stance
Official: the Defence Minister said the show displayed the IAF's valour and might, and the IAF chief says the force is "fully committed to jointness and integration". Inference: the public message is that the IAF supports integration but wants a design shaped by Indian needs rather than a copy of foreign models; no government decision on the structure was found.
Competing viewpoints
The IAF argues that fixed splits would dilute centralised control of scarce fighter assets. Proponents of theatre commands argue that dedicated assets would improve joint planning and accountability; those arguments come from earlier public debate rather than from the 25 Oct coverage, which carries only the IAF's view.

SSB cue: "Jointness is a goal, structure is the debate; say that India should integrate the three services through phased, tested reform while keeping air power flexible across theatres."

04 / India · Economy & Cyber Security

PM inaugurates RBI's ₹169 crore data centre and cybersecurity training institute near Bhubaneswar, warning that economies are now targets in war

In ~50 words: On 25 October PM Modi inaugurated the Reserve Bank of India's new data centre and cybersecurity training institute at Infovalley-II, Jatni, near Bhubaneswar. Business Standard puts the cost at ₹169 crore. He said wars are no longer fought with weapons alone and that a growing economy must shield its banking system from "cyber looters and cyber terrorists".

What happened?
The data centre is meant to support RBI's mission-critical payment and settlement systems with business continuity during natural disasters and geopolitical risks. The training institute will cover cybersecurity, enterprise computing and emerging fields such as AI and quantum computing (Akashvani, Business Standard). The PM said the previous decade focused on banking the unbanked and the next will make banking more efficient and reliable.
Why it matters
Payments and settlement are national critical infrastructure; a second major site in the east adds geographic redundancy. It also links to the brief's theme that modern pressure targets networks, as in the reported Iranian cable threats.
India's stance
Official: the PM described cyber defence of the financial system as a national priority and framed it as protecting a rising economic power. Inference: this is a capacity-building step; the sources reviewed do not give details of any specific threat or incident behind the timing.
Competing viewpoints
Supporters see resilience and skills creation in a state that is also facing a cyclone this week. Sceptics would note that infrastructure is only one part of defence and that talent retention, bank-level security and incident reporting matter equally; that view is an inference, not drawn from the reports.

SSB cue: "Economic security is national security; say that India should build redundant financial infrastructure, train its own cyber specialists, and treat banks' cyber hygiene as seriously as border defence."

Sources: Business Standard, 25 Oct; Akashvani (AIR News), 25 Oct.

05 / India · Law & Judiciary

Ex-SC judge Sanjay Kishan Kaul's memoir alleges ex-CJI Chandrachud was "too deferential" to the government on judicial appointments

In ~50 words: Excerpts around Justice Kaul's forthcoming memoir 'Without Fear or Favour' (Juggernaut, due later this month) say he objected to names being discussed with the government before the collegium formally deliberated. He also says the collegium is "deeply imperfect". The claims are Justice Kaul's and are unadjudicated.

What happened?
Justice Kaul, who sat on the collegium from June 2022 to December 2023, recounts a meeting to fill six vacancies where five names were agreed and then then-CJI Chandrachud mooted a sixth and wondered aloud whether to check the government's view. He distinguishes consultation from "pre-approval". The Economic Times reports legal experts split: senior advocate Vikas Singh backed the criticism, while another expert defended Chandrachud's tenure. No response from Justice Chandrachud or the Centre was found in the sources reviewed.
Why it matters
The collegium's opacity and its relationship with the executive have been contested since the NJAC verdict. A former member's account from inside the room adds first-hand detail to a long-running debate on judicial independence and accountability.
India's stance
Official: no government response was found. Inference: the government's long-held position that the collegium lacks transparency may be cited by both sides.
Competing viewpoints
Kaul argues the collegium must resist executive pressure and that earlier practice sets a precedent for later chiefs with shorter tenures. Defenders of Chandrachud say, per the ET report, that he served on the administrative and judicial sides with independence; Kaul himself says Chandrachud had intellectual depth and capacity on the judicial side.

SSB cue: "Judicial independence needs both institutional distance and transparency; say that India should publish clearer appointment criteria and timelines instead of resolving the debate through personalities."

06 / India · Diplomacy & BRICS

Iran's parliament speaker Ghalibaf skips the BRICS Parliamentary Forum in New Delhi, citing "unforeseen circumstances"

In ~50 words: Iran's embassy said on 24 October that Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, also Tehran's chief negotiator with the US, will not attend the 12th BRICS Parliamentary Forum in New Delhi on 26–27 October. A parliamentary delegation will still attend. The embassy gave no further explanation; media link it to the risk of renewed US-Israeli strikes.

What happened?
Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla had confirmed Ghalibaf's attendance days earlier, per the Economic Times. The embassy thanked India for its hospitality and reaffirmed Iran's commitment to BRICS participation. NewsCord's summary of The Hindu says Iranian officials also pressed for a bilateral air route for humanitarian aid, and that India's position is that medicines can reach Iran through third countries such as Armenia and Oman; I could not verify that report directly.
Why it matters
The forum brings parliamentary leaders of BRICS members together as the grouping struggles to speak with one voice on the Iran war. A top Iranian negotiator staying away shows how the conflict is reshaping diplomatic calendars.
India's stance
Official: India is hosting the forum and the Lok Sabha Speaker chairs it; there was no Indian comment on the absence in the sources reviewed. Inference: India will try to keep the forum on its stated agenda and avoid being pulled into US-Iran disputes while preserving ties with Tehran.
Competing viewpoints
Some outlets tie the absence to security concerns about the war resuming after the US midterms; the embassy says only "unforeseen circumstances". Others see it as a signal of unhappiness with India's pace on humanitarian channels, which is unconfirmed.

SSB cue: "Multilateral platforms are only as strong as their members' attendance; say that India should keep BRICS focused on shared interests such as reform and trade while keeping channels open to all members."

07 / India · Diplomacy & Multilateralism

Jaishankar says the UN must "show leadership, not be a spectator"; at the UNGA an Indian diplomat calls Pakistan's army the "real rulers"

In ~50 words: At a UN Day event in Delhi on 25 October, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar urged the UN to lead on crises and said terrorism can never be justified. A day earlier at the UN General Assembly's Third Committee, India's Raghoo Puri rejected Pakistan's criticism, calling its civilian government a "Form 47 government" subordinate to the army.

What happened?
Jaishankar, speaking at the Chinmaya Amrit Mahotsav Global Sammelan, called for reformed multilateralism, said large parts of the world cannot be excluded from decision-making, and said dialogue and diplomacy are the only solution to wars; he added that using terrorism as statecraft is "unacceptable". At the Third Committee dialogue with the Special Rapporteur on Minority Issues on Saturday, Puri said Pakistan was deflecting from its economy and rights record and reiterated that Jammu and Kashmir is an integral part of India. "Form 47" is a term critics use to question Pakistan's 2024 election results, which is contested language.
Why it matters
It shows India pairing a reform-the-UN message with a sharper counter to Pakistan's use of UN forums, and links the UN's credibility to wars in Ukraine and West Asia.
India's stance
Official: India says it is "making efforts" for peace and is open to working with partners; it treats Kashmir as an internal matter and the only outstanding issue as Pakistan's illegal occupation. These are statements of position.
Competing viewpoints
Pakistan frequently raises Kashmir and minority treatment in India at the UN, which India rejects. A general caveat, not from these reports, is that Security Council expansion needs agreement among members who have not agreed for decades.

SSB cue: "Credibility comes from consistency; say that India should argue for UN reform and zero tolerance for terror while backing its words with peacekeeping, aid and rule-based conduct."

08 / India · Disaster Management

Bay of Bengal deep depression set to become a very severe cyclonic storm; Andhra Pradesh and Odisha prepare for landfall on 28 October

In ~50 words: IMD said on 25 October that a deep depression over the Bay of Bengal would intensify into a cyclonic storm by 26 October and a very severe cyclonic storm before crossing the Andhra Pradesh coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam on the forenoon of 28 October, with winds of 115–125 kmph, gusting to 140 kmph. Odisha has also mobilised response teams.

What happened?
The system was a depression at 5:30 am and a deep depression by 11:30 am on 25 October, per IMD bulletins; it was later named Cyclone Arnab on 26 October (Hindustan Times). IMD forecast extremely heavy rain over coastal Andhra Pradesh on 27–28 October and Odisha on 28 October. Odisha shut schools and Anganwadi centres in five districts for 28–29 October, placed 60 ODRAF, 17 NDRF and 225 fire-service teams on alert, and postponed the President's visit to 4–5 November (Indian Express).
Why it matters
Eastern coastal states face repeated cyclones; early forecasts and evacuations have cut deaths in recent decades, but very heavy rain and wind still threaten crops, power and fisherfolk.
India's stance
Official: IMD advised fishermen not to venture into the sea, and the Andhra Pradesh government reiterated a "zero-casualty objective" (The Hindu live, PTI). Inference: the real test comes after landfall, in restoring power and relief; results are not yet known.
Competing viewpoints
Officials stress preparedness and timely warnings. Some experts argue that the focus should shift from evacuation alone to resilient infrastructure and coastal planning; that is a general view, not drawn from the 25 Oct reports.

SSB cue: "Disasters test coordination more than courage; say that India should keep forecasting, early evacuation and NDRF-state-local coordination ready, and rebuild with climate-resilient design."

10 / International · Russia–Ukraine

Russia and Ukraine agree a local humanitarian ceasefire in occupied Oleshky from 27 October, brokered via Melania Trump; peace talks planned for next month

In ~50 words: Ukrainian, Russian and US negotiators agreed on 23 October a ceasefire around Russian-occupied Oleshky, Kherson region, to allow aid and evacuations. Ukraine's General Staff says it starts 3 a.m. Kyiv time on Tuesday 27 October. Zelenskiy said negotiators will meet next month. Many details have not been released.

What happened?
The 24 Oct brief did not carry this item. AP reports the town has been without power, heat or water, and that US first lady Melania Trump brokered the deal; officials on all sides confirmed it. The ceasefire covers the Kherson region and neighbouring areas needed for the operation, per Ukraine's human-rights commissioner Lubinets. It is a local pause, not a general ceasefire.
Why it matters
A small, verifiable humanitarian truce could be a confidence-building step before talks; it comes as AP reports tensions between the Trump administration and Kyiv over restrictions on the Starshield satellite internet system.
India's stance
Official: Jaishankar says India favours dialogue and diplomacy and is willing to work with partners (see item 07); no India-specific comment on Oleshky was found. Inference: India benefits from any easing of the war through energy prices, fertiliser and grain supply chains.
Competing viewpoints
Supporters see a humanitarian opening. Sceptics note that Putin has shown few signs of compromising on core aims such as full control of Donetsk and no NATO membership for Kyiv (Reuters, earlier), and that a local truce can break down quickly. Both are assessments.

SSB cue: "Small steps matter in long wars; say that India should welcome humanitarian truces, offer relief support where asked, and keep pressing for a durable settlement through talks."

12 / India · Education & Technology

NCERT releases a 20-hour AI and computational-thinking module for classes 9 to 12, with deepfake detection and data privacy

In ~50 words: NCERT has released an activity-based, 20-hour introductory module for students in classes 9–12, developed with IIT Madras and IIT Jodhpur. It covers everyday AI, how machines learn, deepfake detection, AI limitations such as hallucinations, personal-data protection and responsible use. Activities can be done with or without devices, so low-connectivity schools can take part.

What happened?
NCERT announced the module in a press release on Friday (23 Oct), and PTI-based reports appeared on 25 Oct. Hindustan Times reports it is for students with no prior AI knowledge and offers three modes: a device per student or pair, teacher-led demonstrations on a shared screen, and "unplugged" paper exercises. Moneycontrol says lessons caution against entering Aadhaar, PAN, passwords or OTPs into AI tools. It is a module released for use, not a report on its rollout.
Why it matters
It puts verification and ethics, not only tool use, into school learning at a time of rising AI-generated misinformation and digital fraud.
India's stance
Official: NCERT says the module reinforces that "human judgement, creativity and ethical responsibility remain central". Inference: it fits India's wider push for AI skills; the sources reviewed do not say which states or boards will adopt it or when.
Competing viewpoints
Supporters welcome early AI literacy. Sceptics, as a general concern and not from the reports, would ask whether schools have trained teachers and enough time in the timetable, and whether 20 hours can build real verification habits.

SSB cue: "AI literacy is a civic skill; say that India should teach students to question, verify and protect their data, and train teachers before expanding the curriculum."

Vani Kapoor ends second at the Hero Women's Indian Open, her best Ladies European Tour finish; Harmanpreet and Deepti hit hundreds against Zimbabwe

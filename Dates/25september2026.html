01 / India · Defence & Maritime Security

ICG Ship Shaunak sails for Perth in Quad-at-Sea observer mission

In ~50 words: Indian Coast Guard Ship Shaunak departed Chennai on 25 September for Perth, Australia, launching the Quad-at-Sea Ship Observer Mission. Six sea riders—two each from India and Japan, and one each from Australia and the US—are embarked for joint shipboard drills. The mission operationalises coast-guard level interoperability and reinforces India's MAHASAGAR regional maritime security framework.

What happened?
ICGS Shaunak sailed from Chennai on 25 September for Perth, Australia, carrying multinational observers to conduct joint maritime exercises and professional exchanges under the Quad framework.
Why it matters
It moves the Quad from high-level diplomatic statements to operational white-hull (coast guard) cooperation, enhancing maritime domain awareness and search-and-rescue across the Indo-Pacific without creating a formal military pact.
India's stance
India aligns the mission with its MAHASAGAR doctrine and commitment to a free, open, and rules-based Indo-Pacific, demonstrating functional maritime leadership in the Indian Ocean.
Competing viewpoints
Advocates welcome coordinated deterrence against illegal maritime activities and grey-zone coercion; critics, especially China, view Quad maritime patrols as containment architecture disguised as civilian policing.

SSB cue: Distinguish white-hull (Coast Guard) diplomacy from naval warfighting; explain how Quad maritime cooperation builds interoperability while preserving India's strategic autonomy.

04 / International · Trade & Climate Policy

India and 17 nations reserve third-party rights in WTO dispute on EU CBAM

In ~50 words: India, the US, China, and 15 other nations reserved third-party rights at the World Trade Organization after the Dispute Settlement Body agreed to establish an adjudicating panel on Russia's challenge against the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). The dispute challenges unilateral cross-border carbon tariffs as discriminatory non-tariff trade barriers.

What happened?
At Geneva on 25 September, the WTO Dispute Settlement Body established a formal dispute panel on Russia's complaint against the EU carbon tax, with India and 17 key economies formally reserving third-party participatory rights.
Why it matters
The ruling will establish global trade jurisprudence on whether unilateral climate levies constitute legitimate environmental regulation or disguised protectionism that penalises developing nations' industrial exports.
India's stance
India opposes unilateral carbon taxes that undermine the UNFCCC principle of Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (CBDR), while simultaneously creating its own domestic Carbon Credit Trading Scheme.
Competing viewpoints
The EU argues CBAM prevents "carbon leakage" and levels the playing field for decarbonised industries; developing nations contend it acts as a protectionist tariff that unfairly exports European emission costs onto external producers.

SSB cue: Connect green trade measures to Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (CBDR); distinguish genuine climate action from unilateral protectionist trade barriers.

What happened?
The US administration formally cleared the 48-aircraft F-35 package for Riyadh, subject to Congressional review, featuring tailored avionics distinct from Israel's proprietary F-35I Adir fleet.
Why it matters

India's stance
India maintains strategic partnerships with both Riyadh and Tel Aviv; a stable, non-escalatory balance in the Persian Gulf is critical for India's 9 million diaspora and primary crude energy imports.
Competing viewpoints
Washington views the sale as essential to reassure Gulf partners and deter Iran; critics caution that proliferating fifth-generation stealth jets risks a qualitative regional arms race and unsettles Israeli defence planning.

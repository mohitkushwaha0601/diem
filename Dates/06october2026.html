01 / India · Economy

RBI keeps policy stance steady as inflation cools but growth and liquidity concerns persist

In ~50 words: The Reserve Bank of India kept its policy rate unchanged on 6 October, signalling confidence that inflation was softening without any need to force a broader monetary tightening. The real takeaway was that India was balancing the need to preserve disinflation with the risk of slowing credit growth and private demand.

What happened?
Since the 5 October brief, the RBI continued to signal a measured stance, with the policy committee emphasising that price pressures were moderating but uneven across food and services. The message remained one of patience: the central bank did not need to move sharply on either side, but it was still watching liquidity conditions and growth momentum closely.
Why it matters
Rate policy is one of the clearest ways a government signals confidence in the economy. A steady stance matters because it influences lending, business confidence and the cost of capital, especially when inflation is falling but consumers and firms still need support.
India's stance
India’s macro stance was to avoid both panic tightening and premature easing. The RBI’s logic was that maintaining credibility matters more than responding to short-term market noise, especially if inflation is still uneven across categories.
Competing viewpoints
Hawks argued that the bank should continue guarding against inflation persistence, while growth supporters said it needed to keep credit flowing to sustain investment and jobs. The dispute was essentially about how much policy flexibility India could preserve without compromising inflation control.

SSB cue: “India is not chasing either extreme; it is trying to keep growth alive while defending the credibility of its inflation framework.”

01 / India · Economy

RBI holds repo rate as inflation cools but growth and liquidity concerns keep policy hawkish

In ~50 words: The Reserve Bank of India kept the repo rate unchanged on 5 October, even as markets had priced in a sharper move earlier in the week. The policy balance remained between easing growth pressure and guarding against sticky food and services inflation, which continued to shape the central bank’s credibility and transmission strategy.

What happened?
Since the 4 October brief, the RBI Monetary Policy Committee met and held the repo rate at the previous level, while maintaining a measured tone on inflation and liquidity. The statement highlighted that the disinflation trend was uneven, especially in food, while the growth outlook remained resilient enough to avoid an aggressive tightening cycle.
Why it matters
The rate decision influences interest costs for households, corporates and the government, and it signals how much policy space India has for growth support. For SSB relevance, the key point is that macro policy is not just about a number—it is a trade-off between price stability, credit flow and job creation.
India's stance
India’s policy stance was to maintain price stability without choking the real economy. The central bank’s message reinforced that it would prefer calibrated credibility over sudden easing or aggressive tightening, even if that creates friction for borrowers and markets.
Competing viewpoints
Hawks argued the bank needed a firmer stance to contain inflation persistence, while growth advocates said the economy still required room for investment and consumption. The disagreement was less about the headline rate and more about how much policy flexibility India should preserve.

SSB cue: “India is trying to keep inflation under control without losing the growth engine; that is the real policy challenge behind the RBI decision.”
